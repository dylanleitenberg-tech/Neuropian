Privacy Policy

Last updated: 29 October 2026

1. Who we are

Neuropian is run by Dylan Leitenberg (not yet incorporated). Questions and requests: dylan@neuropian.com.

2. What we collect from members

  • Account: name, username, email address, date of birth, profile picture and your Neuropian ID.
  • Country: the country you live in, which you tell us when you sign up or publish a profile. Until you tell us, we may fill it in from the country of the connection you signed up from, the location on your public GitHub profile or your email address's country domain. We use it only to know which privacy laws apply to you (for example, whether you are in the EU), and it is never shown on your profile.
  • Sign-in with GitHub or another service: your username, name, email address and picture from that service, and the public work there (for GitHub, your public repositories and the pull requests other projects merged), which Neuropian may read to build your profile.
  • Profile: your bio, and the answers you gave in our earlier profile survey, which we no longer ask.
  • RoundTable: messages, files you upload, reactions, problems and builds you post, servers, friends, reports and whether you are online.
  • Opportunities: what you save or apply to.
  • Credits and projects: your credit balance and history, projects you post or ask to join, the proof of work you submit, and reviewers' decisions.
  • Public profile, if you make one: what you write or approve (name, headline, summary, skills, work and links), and, if you connect GitHub, your GitHub username and what GitHub shares at sign-in (name, email address and profile picture).
  • A profile we make after you say yes by email: your email address, your reply (including that you are 18 or over) and the public work we built the profile from.
  • Our assessment of your public work: an internal rating we use to decide whom to contact and whom to recommend to companies. It is never shown on your profile, published or shown to companies.
  • EagleEyes, if you use it: on the sites you turn it on for, what you read, write and engage with there. Nothing from any other site.
  • Technical: what our hosting provider logs to run sign-in securely, such as IP address and browser.

3. How we use it

To run your account and RoundTable, to match you with opportunities, to share your profile with companies and funds hiring through Neuropian (section 4), to keep the service secure, and to send account emails such as confirmations and password resets. No marketing email without your consent.

4. Who we share it with

  • Companies and funds hiring through Neuropian: your profile (name, username, answers to our old survey if you gave them, and what you add to your profile) when you may fit what they are looking for. We give a company your email address only when you agree to be introduced to it. They may pay us. Under California law this can count as selling or sharing personal information. You can opt out at any time: email dylan@neuropian.com with “Do not share my profile” and we stop within 7 days.
  • Service providers that run the service for us: Supabase (database, sign-in and file storage, in the United States), Cloudflare (the website) and Google Workspace (our email). Pages also load fonts from Google Fonts and code from jsDelivr, which see your IP address.
  • If the law requires it.

Never shared with hiring companies: your password and your private messages. Never sold to data brokers or advertisers.

Everyone can see and search a profile you publish on Talent: the headline, summary, skills, work and links you approved, your GitHub username if you connected GitHub, and your name only if you chose to show it. To write the draft, your public work (such as your GitHub activity or papers) may be sent to Anthropic's Claude AI model; search uses an AI model that runs inside Supabase.

Other members can see projects, who posted them, and approved contributors with the proof that was approved. Requests to join and work waiting for review are seen only by the member who sent it, the project's owner and Neuropian reviewers.

Other members can see your username, name, profile picture, Neuropian ID, whether you are online, your bio and profile-card answers (unless you set them to friends only) and the messages you post in rooms and servers.

Accounts created before 18 October 2026 were made under a policy that did not include sharing. Nothing from those accounts is shared until the member accepts these terms.

5. People we find through public work

Neuropian's search tool, Falcon, reads public, self-published information through official data feeds and public pages: GitHub (profiles, merged pull requests, commits and code search), Codeforces, AtCoder, Lichess, Chess.com, Math Stack Exchange and other Stack Exchange sites, Kaggle's public Meta Kaggle dataset, the Online Go Server, Hacker News, arXiv and the papers posted there, OpenAlex (paper and citation records), and personal websites linked from those profiles. We use a site's official data feed where it has one, and check robots.txt before fetching any other page.

What it keeps: usernames; the name, location, company, bio and website shown on public profiles; follower counts; contest results, answers and merged pull requests; a short fingerprint of public profile photos, used only to tell whether two accounts belong to the same person (not the photo itself); and contact details people published themselves, such as a public profile email, the email printed on their paper or the email and social-media links on their own website. For a few people we also use the email recorded on their public commits, which they may not have meant to publish; when we do, our email says so.

From this we keep lists and short internal reports. We publish only totals and one redacted example, never anyone's name or profile unless they said yes to one, and we share no one's information with a hiring company unless they have an account or said yes to a profile. For someone who said yes by email, a company sees only that public profile, and gets their email address only if they agree to be introduced. We may email someone whose work fits to ask whether they would like a public profile. If they say no or ask us to stop, we don't write again, and we never publish a profile without a yes. To see what we hold about you or to have it removed, email dylan@neuropian.com.

6. Companies and funds that contact us

When you tell us about a role at neuropian.com/hire or by email, we keep your name, work email, company, website and the role details you send (such as type, location, pay and stack), and copy them to our own inbox so we can reply. We use them only to find members who fit and to talk with you about the role. We don't sell them or add you to marketing lists. To see or delete them, email dylan@neuropian.com.

7. Storage and how long we keep it

Account data is stored with Supabase in the United States, encrypted in transit and at rest. Falcon's lists and our working files are kept on our own encrypted computer, and email, including the roles companies send us, is kept in Google Workspace. How long we keep it: account data while your account is open, deleted within 30 days of your request to close it; a profile made after you said yes by email, until you ask us to remove it; Falcon's records about people we find, while they help us find people for roles, and deleted when the person asks; role details from companies, while we work with you on hiring and for up to 2 years after, unless you ask us to delete them sooner.

8. Your rights

You can ask to see, correct, download or delete your data, or to stop sharing, by emailing dylan@neuropian.com. We answer within 30 days. If you are in the EU or UK: for members, the legal basis is the service you sign up for, which is being matched with hiring companies; for people Falcon finds, it is our legitimate interest in finding people through work they made public, and you may object at any time. You may also complain to your data-protection authority.

9. Age

Neuropian is for adults (18+). Before 18 October 2026 members could join from 13; those accounts are paused, nothing from them is shared, and we are deleting them. If we learn we hold data from anyone else under 18, we don't share it and we delete it.

10. Changes

If this policy changes in a way that matters, we will ask you to accept it again.
