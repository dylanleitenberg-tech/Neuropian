Neuropian · Legal

Privacy Policy

Last updated: 25 October 2026 · Open as a page

1. Who we are

Neuropian is run by Dylan Leitenberg (not yet incorporated). Questions and requests: dylan@neuropian.com.

2. What we collect from members

  • Account: name, username, email address, date of birth, profile picture and your Neuropian ID.
  • Country: the country you live in, which you tell us when you sign up or publish a profile. For a few accounts that connected GitHub, we filled it in from the location on the public GitHub profile until the member tells us. We use it only to know which privacy laws apply to you (for example, whether you are in the EU), and it is never shown on your profile.
  • Sign-in with GitHub or another service: your username, name, email address and picture from that service, and the public work there (for GitHub, your public repositories and the pull requests other projects merged), which Neuropian may read to build your profile.
  • Profile: your bio, and the answers you gave in our earlier profile survey, which we no longer ask.
  • RoundTable: messages, files you upload, reactions, problems and builds you post, servers, friends, reports and whether you are online.
  • Opportunities: what you save or apply to.
  • Credits and projects: your credit balance and history, projects you post or ask to join, the proof of work you submit, and reviewers' decisions.
  • Public profile, if you make one: what you write or approve (name, headline, summary, skills, work and links), and, if you connect GitHub, your GitHub username and what GitHub shares at sign-in (name, email address and profile picture).
  • EagleEyes, if you use it: on the sites you turn it on for, what you read, write and engage with there. Nothing from any other site.
  • Technical: what our hosting provider logs to run sign-in securely, such as IP address and browser.

3. How we use it

To run your account and RoundTable, to match you with opportunities, to share your profile with companies and funds hiring through Neuropian (section 4), to keep the service secure, and to send account emails such as confirmations and password resets. No marketing email without your consent.

4. Who we share it with

  • Companies and funds hiring through Neuropian: your profile (name, username, email, survey results and what you add to your profile) when you may fit what they are looking for. They may pay us. Under California law this can count as selling or sharing personal information. You can opt out at any time: email dylan@neuropian.com with “Do not share my profile” and we stop within 7 days.
  • Service providers that run the service for us: Supabase (database, sign-in and file storage, in the United States) and Cloudflare (the website). Pages also load fonts from Google Fonts and code from jsDelivr, which see your IP address.
  • If the law requires it.

Never shared with hiring companies: your password and your private messages. Never sold to data brokers or advertisers.

Everyone can see and search a profile you publish on Talent: the headline, summary, skills, work and links you approved, your GitHub username if you connected GitHub, and your name only if you chose to show it. To write the draft, your public GitHub information may be sent to Anthropic's Claude AI model; search uses an AI model that runs inside Supabase.

Other members can see projects, who posted them, and approved contributors with the proof that was approved. Requests to join and work waiting for review are seen only by the member who sent it, the project's owner and Neuropian reviewers.

Other members can see your username, name, profile picture, Neuropian ID, whether you are online, your bio and profile-card answers (unless you set them to friends only) and the messages you post in rooms and servers.

Accounts created before 18 October 2026 were made under a policy that did not include sharing. Nothing from those accounts is shared until the member accepts these terms.

5. People we find through public work

Neuropian's search tool, Falcon, reads public, self-published information through official data feeds and public pages: GitHub (profiles, merged pull requests and code search), Codeforces, AtCoder, Math Stack Exchange and other Stack Exchange sites, Kaggle's public Meta Kaggle dataset, the Online Go Server, and personal websites linked from those profiles. Every page fetch checks the site's robots.txt first.

What it keeps: usernames; the name, location, company, bio and website shown on public profiles; follower counts; contest results, answers and merged pull requests; a short fingerprint of public profile photos, used only to tell whether two accounts belong to the same person (not the photo itself); and contact details people published themselves, such as a public profile email or the email and social-media links on their own website.

From this we keep lists and short internal reports. We publish only totals and one redacted example, never anyone's name or profile, and we share no one's information with a hiring company unless they have signed up. To see what we hold about you or to have it removed, email dylan@neuropian.com.

6. Storage and how long we keep it

Your data is stored with Supabase in the United States, encrypted in transit and at rest. We keep it while your account is open and delete it within 30 days of your request to close it.

7. Your rights

You can ask to see, correct, download or delete your data, or to stop sharing, by emailing dylan@neuropian.com. We answer within 30 days. If you are in the EU or UK: for members, the legal basis is the service you sign up for, which is being matched with hiring companies; for people Falcon finds, it is our legitimate interest in finding people through work they made public, and you may object at any time. You may also complain to your data-protection authority.

8. Age

Neuropian is for adults (18+). Before 18 October 2026 members could join from 13; those accounts are paused, nothing from them is shared, and we are deleting them. If we learn we hold data from anyone else under 18, we don't share it and we delete it.

9. Changes

If this policy changes in a way that matters, we will ask you to accept it again.

Terms of Service

Last updated: 19 October 2026 · Open as a page

1. Who we are

Neuropian is run by Dylan Leitenberg. The company is not incorporated yet; when it is, these terms move to it and we will tell you. Contact: dylan@neuropian.com.

2. What Neuropian is

Neuropian finds skilled people for companies and funds that are hiring. With an account you can build a profile, browse opportunities and use RoundTable, our community chat.

3. We share your profile with companies that hire through us

This is what Neuropian is for. When you create an account, we may share your profile with companies and funds hiring through Neuropian when you may fit what they are looking for. Your profile means your name, username, email address, survey results and anything you add to your profile. Those companies may contact you, and they may pay Neuropian.

We never share your password or your private messages, and we do not sell your information to data brokers or advertisers. You can turn sharing off at any time by emailing dylan@neuropian.com; we stop within 7 days.

4. Who can join

You must be 18 or older. One account per person, with accurate information. You are responsible for keeping your password safe and for what happens under your account.

5. Rules

Don't post anything harassing, threatening, defamatory, obscene or illegal; don't impersonate anyone; don't try to get into other accounts or our systems; don't send spam or malware; don't scrape or copy other members' content or data; don't break the law. We may remove content or suspend accounts that break these rules, without notice in serious cases.

6. Your content

You own what you post. You let Neuropian store, display and use it to run and improve the service. Don't post other people's confidential information or material you don't have the rights to.

7. RoundTable

Messages in RoundTable rooms and servers can be seen by the members of that room or server. Report anything that breaks these rules from the message menu.

8. EagleEyes

EagleEyes is our browser extension. It is off on every site until you turn it on for that site. On those sites it records what you read, write and engage with and adds it to your Neuropian profile. It is not yet public. Don't turn it on for sites whose rules forbid monitoring tools; you are responsible for that.

9. No promises about results

We don't guarantee a job, an introduction or any other result. The service is provided as it is, and we may change or stop any part of it.

10. Limits on liability

As far as the law allows, Neuropian is not liable for indirect or consequential losses, or for what other members or hiring companies do.

11. Ending

You can delete your account at any time by emailing dylan@neuropian.com. We may suspend or close accounts that break these terms.

12. Changes

If we change these terms in a way that matters, we will ask you to accept them again before you continue.

13. Law

These terms are governed by the laws of the State of Hawaii, USA.

14. Public profiles and Talent search

You can publish a profile to neuropian.com/talent. Neuropian drafts it from what you write, your links and, if you connect GitHub, your public GitHub work, and may use AI to write it; you edit and approve it, and nothing is public until you press Publish. A published profile can be seen and searched by anyone, and you can unpublish it at any time. Only connect a GitHub account that is yours, and only list work you did. Your GitHub username is shown if you connect GitHub, and links to your work usually point to your accounts, so hiding your name may not make you anonymous.

15. Neuropian Credits

Credits are points inside Neuropian. They have no cash value: they can't be bought, sold, traded, moved outside Neuropian or exchanged for money or anything else of value, and they are not pay for work.

You get welcome credits once, after you publish a profile; you also need a published profile to join a project. When you post a project, its reward is held from your credits until the project ends. People who join submit proof of what they did, and a Neuropian reviewer (Neuropian staff, who did not work on the project and gets no credits for reviewing) approves or rejects each person's work. Approved contributors share the reward. Approved contributors and the project's owner can also get a Neuropian bonus. Bonuses have limits to stop abuse: none when a person's share is under 10 credits, at most one between the same owner and contributor every 30 days, and at most one for an owner every 7 days.

If no work is approved, the reward goes back to the owner. An owner can cancel an open project and get the reward back until someone submits work. A Neuropian reviewer can close a project that is stuck in review or that nobody has submitted work to, and an owner can close one that has waited 14 days for review. When a project in review is closed, contributors already approved share the reward, work not yet reviewed earns nothing, and if nobody was approved the reward goes back to the owner.

Any agreement about the work itself is between the members involved. Neuropian may correct mistakes, take back credits gained through fake accounts or projects, and change or end credits. Credits are for adults (18+). Before you first use credits, you accept this section by clicking to accept the credit rules.
