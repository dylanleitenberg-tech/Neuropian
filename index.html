Method

Three kinds of evidence.

01

Hidden reasoners

Contest and math records from Codeforces, AtCoder and Math Stack Exchange, standardized within each platform.

0.655AUC, among players of the same rating, for who reached 2400 within two years. 0.668 when repeated from a 2023 start. 0.5 is chance.

Rating already finds strong players, and our score does not beat it overall. What it adds is a tie-breaker among equally rated players, and that result held at a second start date.

Method and numbers
  • Cohort. Every Codeforces player rated 1600–2300 on 16 Oct 2024, with at least 15 rated contests and 3 in the year before: 11,088 players. 290 (2.6%) reached 2400 in the two years after.
  • Overall. Rating alone ranks them with AUC 0.896 (95% CI 0.878–0.913). Our scout score: 0.879 (0.860–0.896). Rating wins.
  • Among equals. AUC computed inside each 100-point rating band, then averaged, weighted by the number who broke through (bands with fewer than 3 skipped). Score = 12-month rating gain + months with a contest out of the last 12 + distance below the player's own peak − years active − contest count, each standardized: 0.655 for reaching 2400, 0.645 for 2600.
  • Second start date. Same test from 18 Oct 2023 (10,139 players; 364 reached 2400 in three years): 0.668 and 0.699. The same three positive traits were selected.
  • Caveat. At each date, traits were chosen and scored on the same players (kept if within-band AUC > 0.55, reversed if < 0.45). The 2023 run is a replication, not a held-out test.
  • AUC = the probability that a randomly chosen player who broke through is ranked above one who did not.

02

Hidden builders

People whose code maintainers merged into hard open-source projects, but whom few people follow. Identified by that evidence, not predicted.

150people identified, each with 6 or more merged pull requests in the past 12 months and under 150 GitHub followers.

Projects searched: LLVM, CPython, Julia, JAX, NumPy, SciPy, DuckDB, llama.cpp, Rust and tinygrad.

Method and numbers
  • Rules. Three or more merged pull requests in the 12 months to 17 Oct 2026; 150 GitHub followers or fewer; bots, people who have merged others' work, and people whose profile names a large tech company, elite university or top trading firm removed. Half of the 150 have 12 or fewer followers.
  • Merge rights. 135 of the 150 have never merged a pull request in that project. Rust and tinygrad merge through a bot, so for their 15 people this cannot be checked.
  • Why we don't call it prediction. In eight of the projects (not Rust or tinygrad), 2,958 people had merged pull requests between Oct 2023 and Oct 2024 and had never merged one themselves. 302 did so in the two years after, a sign of write access; 276 of them in LLVM. Merged-PR count alone ranks them with AUC 0.646. No other trait (lines changed, pull-request size, months active, recency) added information when chosen on half the projects and tested on the other half. So the list is ranked by count, and it shows the work.

03

Dossiers

For each person, an open-source model running on our own machine reads 15 of their merged pull requests and writes what they work on. Each dossier links the pull requests it draws on, so a recruiter can check any line against the source.

0 of 30dossiers cited a pull request the model was not shown.
Method and numbers
  • Facts by code. Files touched, size, review rounds and reviewer comments are extracted directly from GitHub, not written by the model.
  • Judgment by model. Specialty and summary come from Qwen3 8B running locally. Nothing about a candidate is sent to an outside AI service.
  • Check. Every pull-request number the model cites is compared with the ones it was given. 30 dossiers so far; 0 invented citations.
  • Not yet used. The model's difficulty ratings don't separate people yet, so they are not shown.

Principles

Four rules.

  1. Public, self-published data only.
  2. Adults only.
  3. Nobody's information goes to a client without their opt-in.
  4. No scraping of sites that forbid it.
